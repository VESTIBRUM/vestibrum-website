01 / The nights

You know this hour.

The house is dark. Your baby has been crying for forty minutes. You've fed, changed, swaddled and shushed. You have to be up in a few hours, and you have no idea whether this is normal.

THE SLEEP YOU LOSE

In a study of more than 4,600 parents, first-time mothers lost about an hour of sleep a night in the first three months after birth, and parents' sleep had not fully recovered four to six years later.

THE GUESSWORK

At 3 a.m., every choice feels like a guess. Pick them up or wait? Feed again? Is the noise machine helping or hurting? Advice is everywhere, and none of it is about your baby.

THE SPENDING

So you buy things. A premium smart bassinet. Then sleep coaching. Each one helps for a while, and then you're back at 3 a.m. with a bigger receipt.

Source: Richter D, et al. Long-term effects of pregnancy and childbirth on sleep satisfaction and duration of first-time and experienced mothers and fathers. Sleep. 2019. PubMed

Why doesn't any of it last? ↓
03 / The cliff

Today's fixes soothe.
They don't teach.

Most smart bassinets are built to respond to crying with more motion and more sound. That helps in the moment, but it isn't designed to help your baby need less. Then, around six months, your baby outgrows the bassinet, and the help disappears all at once.

How will we know it works? ↓
06 / What we're measuring

We'll measure before we promise.

Otoli is still in development. We're designing a home study with pediatric sleep researchers to test our ideas with real families. Here is what we plan to measure, and what we hope to learn.

Settling

How long it takes to fall asleep

Minutes from lights-out to sleep, with gentle motion on and with it off.

We hope to learn whether gentle motion helps babies settle sooner.

Laydown

Staying asleep after being put down

Many babies fall asleep in your arms and wake the moment they're laid down.

We hope to learn whether a bassinet that's already moving makes that handoff easier.

The night

Night wakings and the longest stretch of sleep

Measured with overnight video and small wearable movement sensors, not just the bassinet's own sensors.

We hope to learn how nights change as support steps down.

Stepping back

Whether babies come to rely on the motion

How babies sleep without motion before and after a week with it.

We hope to learn whether gradual reduction avoids the dependence parents worry about.

You

Parent sleep and confidence

Your sleep, your stress, and how sure you feel at 3 a.m.

We hope to learn whether a system that explains your baby's nights helps you rest too.

Real life

Whether families actually use it

Setup, comfort, noise, and what parents would change.

We hope to learn what makes Otoli easy to live with.

Our promise

We won't claim Otoli improves sleep until we've measured it. Studies with infants will be reviewed by an independent ethics board before they begin, and we'll share what we learn, including what doesn't work. Otoli is a general wellness product. It is not a medical device and does not monitor breathing or health.
