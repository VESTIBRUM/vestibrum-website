03 / The Science

The mechanism is otolith-mediated, and the intelligence is closed-loop.

The infant vestibular system contains two otolith organs — the saccule and the utricle — that sense linear acceleration. Otoli's defining feature is not the motion that engages them, but the closed-loop intelligence that governs it.

As the infant sleeps, the system continuously senses movement, breathing, and sound, and adapts its calibrated linear motion and acoustic cues in real time. This differs fundamentally from the fixed rotational rocking of motion-based competitors, which run preset patterns and primarily engage the semicircular canals.

"Linear acceleration acting on the otolith organs is sufficient to induce sleep onset in infant mammals."

Kompotis et al., Current Biology (2019)

Most importantly, the same intelligence that soothes also weans. As the baby develops the capacity to self-soothe, Otoli imperceptibly withdraws its assistance — working invisibly, personalizing continuously, ultimately designed to make itself unnecessary.
